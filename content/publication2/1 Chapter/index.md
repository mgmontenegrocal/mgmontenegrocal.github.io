abstract: 'This paper explores how Diagnostic Expectations (DE) modify the state-space structure of linear macroeconomic models and shape forecast errors (FE) and forecast revisions (FR) across horizons. Using a three-equation framework, I derive testable expressions in terms of model parameters and then generalise the analysis to larger models. I show that DE introduce predictability in the form of moving-average (MA) processes. I test this result using survey data from the Philadelphia Fed’s Survey of Professional Forecasters and policymakers’ Greenbook/Tealbook forecasts, which allows for a comparison of expectation formation across agents. The empirical results indicate that one-period-ahead FE generally exhibit the MA patterns implied by DE, with signs of overreaction only for GDP growth and mostly when the post-pandemic period is included. The evidence for FR is more mixed. At longer horizons, FE display autoregressive components that deviate from the DE benchmark, whereas FR align more closely with DE-driven expectations, suggesting stronger revisions in the direction of the shock realisation. While these findings offer some insights into belief formation, they remain far from conclusive.'
